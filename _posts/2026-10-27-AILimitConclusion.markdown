# Introduction
As seen in my archives, I finished the game across 5 days. I thought that it will be a game I am really motivated to play, however it has been 2 months since I booted it up. Therefore I think the appropriate question is to ask why? I have already ready up for a NG+ run, and finished the DLC, gotten some cool weapons, yet we are still in NG.

AI Limit it a soulslike game bringing its own spin to the genre, in this case the mana bar and the stability bar is one and the same. It has many things that people coming over from Sekiro might find famillar, such as a parry, or a lighting dodge that consume the stability unless it is a "perfect" dodge. Put together mechnically the game is fun, so we will discuss what makes the game fun.

# Enjoyable Boss fights
It is no secret that in video games bosses are usually the highlight of any specific stages, and this game does have a few spectacle bosses. The humanoid bosses are by far the most fun, where the best bosses include the Hunter of Bladers(and her secret fight), Loskid the Son as well as Eunomia. They are some of the highlights of the game, the primary reason being they make full use of the stability bar, and feels like a duel. The Hunter of Bladers and Loskid both uses readable attacks that come up fast, and any damage taken is clearly the player fault. They are also varied enough that they expect different strategy. 

Loskid phase 2 allows her to enter the next "phase" when her stability bar gets too high, and at the max phase her attacks hit extremely hard, heavily encouraging me to heavily pressure her and  attempt to get her health as low as possible before the final phase. Ironically it also makes mindless parrying bad, as bringing her down to 0% resets her back to 50%.
Meanwhile the Hunter of Bladers heavily encourage parrying, her attacks are very predictable, however in phase 2 it becomes hectic but still heavily encourage parry, as parrying is able to stop a lot of her attacks. Her extremely high posture bar also allows player to take their time as there is limited incentive to try to rush her down when most of her bar does not affect her attacks. Now we know what makes the game fun, lets go over the stability bar.

# The Stability Bar and combat
I think it is a genuinely nice addition, a problem with a lot of Dark Soul games is that mages are limited by the number of cast or mana. so there is a fixed number of cast. Which means for random encounter I am going to be saving my mana for the one big boss. AI Limit fixes this by tying it to the stability bar, I find myself casting spells to start almost every fight, it also introduces a intresting question. Do I try to keep my stability so I am less vulnurable, or do I sacriface it and let myself be more vulnruable when I am hit.

# Middling Level design
Unfortuantely I feel that while the gameplay is fun... the issue is with everything else. Most stages takes place in the wasteland and various shade of greys, the sewers and a poision swamp. Not exactly intresting destinations or sights. The most intresting place is Hagios Patir, the church HQ, that we end up reusing and visiting 3 seperate times. While the stage design is great, they are not exactly unique. To compare this to another game that also has a mostly wasteland setting, the Surge mostly take place in a single factory, despite this each location have enough to make it stand out. The junkyard is mostly sand and the location we can see the sky, while the center factory is a industrial production floor with rooms that serve different functions. In AI Limits most stages blend into each other, and I have no idea where am I going. The awe inspiring Hagios Patir sorta fade away after seeing it for the third time. Elysium is a nice location that essentially serve as a intermission between the final boss and Loksid, so it is wasted.
One thing is for sure, none of these will be places I am interested in.
# Questionable Enemy Placement and usage
While tactically the level design is great, where it loops back into itself and relies on shortcuts, on the tactical level it is boring. The most common layout seems to be a ranged guy and a melee guy, which gets boring after a while. There is also a notable reuse of enemies, near the end game a lot of enemies are the same hammer guys I seen all the way near the start of the game, and some bosses that is downgraded to elite mook status. There is even a final boss rush against the church elite mooks, which we have been fighting the entire game. Outside of these, the enemies don't exactly inspire confidence or taking advantage of the game unique system.
# The DLC that solves everything
Usually the motivation to replay the game is to refight the bosses, test our new weapons, or go through the stages again. Now from the above obviously I will not be replaying the game for the level design, or the regular stages. The DLC solves this by providing a boss rush mode, it does not include every boss but it includes everyone fun. Most importantly it allows me to refight the bosses at different difficulity level. This is the single most important part of the DLC as this means that I get to completely skip doing the levels again and just fight the bosses,aka the fun part. The DLC also comes with a roguelite mode but I feel that for that it isn't exactly fun, as with the right combiantion the player is essentially invincible. So when the DLC provides ALL the focus on gameplay, I end up just doing the DLC. There are some faults such as certain fun bosses that are DLC specific are not included in the DLC boss rush, but it is more than sufficient
# Conclusion
So taking everything into account, the reason why I am having issue going through the game again is that...the stage design isn't exactly fun. Additionally most bosses are middling, I am not exactly anticipating to rock up with a full loadup to fight a reaper, or the dozen or so random mooks that arent fun to fight. On the other hand I can satisfy my need for the combat just with the DLC Boss Arena, yes there is the true ending I am missing but the classic dark souls problem arises where it is so obscure I probably need a guide.
