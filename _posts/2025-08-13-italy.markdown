## Afternoon
### Venice
I heard a lot about Venice, both people claiming it is a tourist trap and that it is beautiful. To be honest I am visiting because in the video game Final Fantasy XV there is a location that is inspired by Venice. I recall that Singapore used to teach that the reason for Venice call is due to complacency, but on second thought it seems like Singapore loves bashing those who can't defend itself to prop themselves up. So I want to see how true this is.

Once I arrive, I think my mind was blown as I saw the Chiesa di San Simeon Piccolo across a river.

I got an unlimited boat pass, then I made my way to Hotel Serenissima. I checked out the Plaza and visited Palazzo Ducale. This led me to the courtyard, art as well as the armoury ending with the prison. Subsequently I went to a convenience store and got some toothpaste, and went back to drink some Venetian wine.

### Palazzo Ducale

The amount of "ceiling" art was really stunning and I did not realize the type of art I am missing until I arrive. There was a painting showing the Pope meeting the Doge and it reminds me of how do people back then record historical moments. Compared to the functional aesthetics I noticed that the fireplace is nicely decorated, with statues of man appearing to lift it up. No doubt these are important figure engraved in the fireplace.

I arrived at the armoury next and there are a lot of weapons. Usually crossbows and flintlocks, I also saw a lot of interesting combinations like a crossbow with a wheellock gun, or a dual barrel gun, also a sword with a gun. It is interesting to see how people back then are also experimenting with what is now known as "inefficient designs" to reach the modern era. I believe this is something I can take back as well, in contrast to Singapore painting itself as getting it right the first time where the leaders are infallible.

After a single right turn I suddenly find myself in the prison cells, the jarring nature of the transition is surprising and how close they keep their enemies nearby. I thought the palace was beautiful, for the first half a day in Venice this is a great impression.

## Morning
### Arsenale di Venezia
For today when I woke up I went to the Arsenale di Venezia.
While waiting for it to open I explored the coastal area and found another water fountain, there was also the Giardini della Marinaressa as well as some other parks and their infrastructure. Subsequently I went and see the guided tour with the Sommergibile Enrico Dandolo, then to the Museo Storico Navale di Venezia. 

### Highlight of the morning
#### Parks and stuff
There is a nice water fountain, I really should get used to it. When walking in the gardens, I noticed when walking around is the lack of cars. No traffic lights the roads are for people and the river are for boats. The peace and quiet makes all the difference. I saw a few interesting art pieces and installations as well.
#### Enrico Dandolo
Now talking about the Arsenale di Venezia I can see why it is still an active military base. The guided tour was interesting and I learnt a bit about Italy during the cold war. This is the first time I had been in a submarine and the interior is really crammed. It is decorated with period appropriate entertainment as well, it is amazing how people survived like this.
#### Museo Storico Navale di Venezia
The naval museums has a lot of nice decorations, but my surprise is how blunt they are about their failures. I saw this majestic ship, the Roma, a pretty large scale model and the information panel stated that it was sunk by the Germans without a single combat experience. The top floor features Korean and Chinese ships, which was surprising that how far historical contact went. Clearly I need to brush up on my history.

## Afternoon
### S. Pietro di Castello
Subsequently I took a boat and drop off at S. Pietro di Castello, a non touristy neighborhood and took in the sights a bit. Then I took another boat and walked around a bit, and found a nice looking building which I visited where it turns out to be the Museo di Storia Naturale Giancarlo Ligabue, before heading for actually good lunch at Bacaro Quebrado.

As it is getting late initially I wanted to visit the Giardini Reali but it isn't open, so I end up going to Correr Museum, as I only have this day left I entered despite being told that part of the museum is already closed due to the time. Then after admiring all the art and statues I went back to my hotel.

### Highlights

I think walking around the neighborhood helps me appreciate that this place is actually rich in history and helps me confirm that Venice is not a tourist trap when even non touristy areas are similar. Also I appreciate the fountain I found.

The most surprising part of the Nature Museum is the dissection. There is an entire corridor of dissected animals which I have never seen before and it is kinda fascinating. I think I was too shocked as I did not remember much about the rest of the exhibits except there is a giant whale at the end.

The Correr Museum confirms more of the same, but beyond all the art I was more appealed by how nice the buildings and pillar look. It is like the entire room is history. I also had fun comparing the different symbols as I walk through each room.

## Morning
### Uffizi

To be honest I only went because the building looks sick. I arrived in the morning and entered without much fuss except a mild annoyance I accidentally bought from a third party reseller instead of the official gallery. I think the website called themself the official guide or something which I mistook as the gallery. I gotta say it is not just the art, it is the atmosphere of the building. I feel like I am living in history, as I walk the white black tilted marbled floor with a golden tiled roof. 

Another thing not seen in my home country is nudity, I think it helped me see things in a different way. In my home country it is one of those forbidden topics that everyone secretly indulges in, but over here it is being used to celebrate the human form, I think I came to appreciate the details in the body. I wonder where did this appreciation for the human body go in the modern time, or maybe Singapore is just prudish.

One art that stuck with me in particular is Judith Slaying Holofernes, something about the woman's expression sells the scene of someone on a mission against Holofernes who is struggling in vain.

There was a gallery for Eroticism, I think the expression transcends what I commonly see as commercial pornography. I think it goes back to the ideal of the beauty of a human body. At least having such an abundance and their history proves that humans have always been interested in sex. Even without the mythological factor, they are still interested in sex. At least I believe that this does a good job at showcasing how morality and what is acceptable seems to be quite fluid, it used to be acceptable in ancient sculptures, then now it isn't. I wonder what is it that causes this change.

### Palazzo Pitti
I visited the Palazzo Pitti next, I recall there is a lot of art in the ceiling that I was looking up most of the time. The highlight is the gardens, it is visible in the palace. There is something special at walking through these gardens that were once meant for rich people. The top of the garden provides a beautiful view of Florence.

The most memorable experience is the several of us trying to figure out how does a water dispenser work, where it turns out that they get it to spurt via covering half of the hole with their thumb. I was so used to looking for a button or some contraptions and was completely blindsided by the physics-based solution.
