# Introduction
Railgun Warning is the most common sound that will ring in my head after playing this, replacing the missile warning. As someone who is introduced to the series via ace combat 7 and fell in love with it, getting all achievements for it, I was highly anticipating Ace Combat 8. At of writing this reflection, I have played the game twice, once on Elite and once on Ace, as well as dabbled a little bit in the multiplayer.

# The First Campaign

Mission 1 to 4 are mostly tutorial missions, they are relatively simple with a generous amount of time, is relatively straightforward in terms of execution and seems to exist to settle the player in. Most enemy attack one at a time, while your escort targets can take a beating before going down. Mission 3 serve as a introduction to the Sotoan air Force, the Lux Lunae and their hierarchy, which we will face for the rest of the game. In terms of difficulty they are not really that remarkable. While Mission 4 is the first mission with ground attacks, and a tunnel the player can fly through and earn the reactions of their wingman.

However, Mission 5, the retaking of Theve is the first real mission and a step up in terms of challenge. You are tasked with protecting the Landing Craft as they attempt to land at the city Theve, where there are multiple objective that all opposes one another. Do you try to take down the attacker planes while the Howitzers bombard your allies, do you fly inland to take out the Howitzers and give enemy air superiority, or do you take down the SAM sites to save yourself while your allies get bombed to kingdom's come. It feels overwhelming with so much going on when the objectives are far apart from each other, that makes it even greater when the player is able to slowly clear out the target without the Landing Craft sinking. Then a mission updates arrive that switches things up, now the Land Battleship is bombarding your forces and everyone is retreating, as you fanatically weaves between the massive airburst explosions while trying to shoot down the enemy jets. By the end I feel that I have really pulled off the impossible and lived up to the name I inherited.

Subsequently Mission 6 follows up with this trend, where despite your allies Queen flight being supposed to protect the southern unit while you escort the northern units, you end up pulling double duty anyways. Mission 7 is the first score attack mission, where the players have a limited time to destroy the enemy assets. I found it rather generous however and never came close to failing. Mission 8 brings us back to Theve and this is a very busy mission that is even more difficult than Mission 5 in the same way but more extreme. Instead of being turned back via a retreat order, your force make it to land and you need to ensure that they are not destroyed. The primary challenge here is not running out of ammo.

Mission 9 serve as the supposed climax to the single digit missions, as we go against the Land Battleship Xanthos, one of the superweapon of the enemy. It has an imposing introduction where its railgun strike part the skies, through it is closer to a cinematic setpiece than gameplay. Historically the rockets are kinda bad so I brought it along, and I am happy to say they made the ground attack weapons useful. In fact it was so useful that I destroyed all land targets a minute into the mission, including both land AEGIS with a single rocket salvo. I end up flying around the city while waiting for my allies to get on with it and dodging the occasional main battleship shots. In my opinion there isn't enough keeping the player occupied.

Subsequently most missions follow the same structure until Mission 14, where I get shot down and need to fly a piece of brick through radar screen. Mission 15 the Wings of Wax is the next difficulty spike, while my AWCAS claim we saw them in Faith Park(Mission 7) I don't remember seeing them at all. However the premise is that we need to take down two supersonic bomber with a powerful ECM that prevent lock on further than 2km. They can also kill your entire escort target in 3 pass. This missions teaches more on positioning and interception instead of chasing. Where the bomber needs to slow down before turning, but is absurdly durable. Once the first jet is destroyed the second jet goes all out and activate CWIS and VLS. The skillset this mission require is not really seen in the previous missions, where this mission is purely on interception skills.

Afterwards mission 16 introduces player to flying low to look at the cargo ship to perform inspection, mission 17 involve destroying a prototype land battleship, while mission 18 serve as an introduction to the higher ranks of the shadows. Shadow 22 also shows up here through it is a minor scene. 

Mission 19 shows up of absolutely nowhere with Land Battleship number 2. Compared to the first, this actually feels closer to a fight that is fun to play. To start it is also invincible with many weapons, the primary difference is that it make use of the surroundings, camping at the base of the space elevator and making it difficult to line up a good shot. It is also a multi stage fight that doesn't purely depends on your allies, we start the mission by clearing the anti air, then we target the barrels of the main gun to prevent them from shooting  down our bombers, which creates the weak spot that we can shoot at to finally win the fight. Where in its final phase it reactive all weapons in a all out desperate attempt, filling the skies with explosions as the well hidden weak sections need to be shot at from the right angle. All of these make it a more engaging fight compared to Land Battleship number 1.

Mission 20 is a power fantasy missions that see us go up against Shadow 99 and 100 which are likely to have far superior equipment than the player, the F22A instead. Compared to the previous mission this is a duel, except the other side have 10 guys that shoot missiles at you. They use the same squad tactics that I am used to, such as shooting at you while you are being chased and attempting to overwhelm the player. The difficulity comes from the enemy better aircraft that can turn faster, making a usual turn fighting a terrible idea. The quantity also track the player awareness of their surroundings to not get pincered. This mission sells the fantasy well and the difficulty make the victory feel earned.

Unfortunately the subsequent missions are not that notable, we get a defense mission on 21,we get a heavy airborne carrier to shoot down at mission 22, and multiple destruction missions. Even mission 27 where we destroy the Megafloat feels strangely easy as a single cruise missile can cause an entire section to collapse, ending with two relatively short tunnel run.

Mission 28 is essentially the pre-ultimate mission featuring 1000 enemies, an extremely long length and a very difficult final fight. The first segment is the purest form of what Ace Combat is about, air combat with essentially infinite ammo. It features a lot of different enemies with bombers as prioity targets. Segment 2 shift to taking air supeority. While the final segment includes the boss fight against Shadow 22 and his wingman, the one who first shot down the player. 

Shadow 22 and his unit is the final boss of mission 28 and truely live up to the name of witchcraft, these guys are the hardest fight despite not having health gates. What makes the fight hard comes down to two things. Firstly, infintie flares. I swear these guys stole all our flares at the start of the war which is why I get about 2 flares, while they seems to flare every few seconds until I run out of time. They use the same wingman tactics as well so even if Shadow 22 flare is on cooldown, his wingman can scatter flares behind him. The second part of why he is so difficult is the insane mobility, he can pull a PSM and fire from his six at a dime and making hard right turns keeping all the momentum. Now there is 4 of them, and this makes the mission one of the hardest so far, so hard that I ran out of time a few time and got E rank for this mission, the lowest possible rank. I end up using machine guns on everyone, I never landed a single hit. This does sell to us how insanely skilled the opponent is, and make his title well deserved. This was a really enjoyable fight even if I think his plane is magic.

Mission 29 is the fight against Moon 11, labelled as the greatest ace of the enemy. She is also using the superjet the X-40 Nightmare. Her fight is mostly cinematic with some health gate. The reason I say this is that she is notably more scripted compare to the chaos of Shadow 22, she is invincible with her drones projecting the anti missile field until a specific line is spoken, and there are scripted scene where she will fly off to railgun the player. The final fight ends with a mini interactive cutscene to finally defeat moon 11.

To end things off, we get mission 30...which is a fight against a random nondescript submarine. I won't lie this is the most boring ending by far, the first few minute is spent shooting at a predictable cruise missile every few seconds or so while the story progresses. Next the submarine finally surfaces and suffer from the same issue where a single bomb annilates most of its defenses and the health gate. I gotta wait for it to dive and surface around 5 times until it actually reveal its weapons. It is a nice send off for sure, through I feel that I will dread playing this.

# The Second Campaign

I will only cover notable changes compared to the original, now we are playing on Ace difficulity that is only unlocked after elite, and we have all the best planes. Firstly Mission 1 to 13 are now completely non factors, the multi weapon carrying unlocked a lot of potential and having the best jets means I get to bring both anti ground and anti air and destroy the mission.

Ironically this make mission 14 even worst, as I have gotten used to flying super maneuverable state of the art fighter jets, so flying this is a struggle. I was also dissapointed to find out there is no hidden shortcuts for a skilled player, so I primary did the exact same thing I did on Elite.

Mission 15 confirms my suspicion that you are not suppose to outrun the jets, even with the fastest planes I cannot catch up to them when they are accelerating. These guys are the only one to keep how hard they are due to being mechanics heavy and relatively durable.

What was unexpected was that Mission 29 is the harder than shadow 22, I suspect that Moon 11 accuracy is greatly boosted, making it very hard to actually dodge it compared to elite which was a non factor. The escort drones also seems better as CWIS and shooting down the player missile. Through it is not as oppressive as shadow 22 as there is only one target compared to 28 where any rando can randomly jump in and attempt to sucker punch me. It feels closer to a duel against a superjet instead of a cutscene.

My worst fears are confirmed as mission 30 is exceptionally boring, and essentially a repeat of the original...

In conclusion, while the game has a lot more missions, with 30 missions in total, I wouldn't say most of them are that memorable. The standout missions would be Missions 5, 8, 19, 28, and 29. The first and second playthroughs are notably different and indicate just how much progression, as well as foresight, can cause player perception to change. Some missions become easier once I am aware of what is going on, such as Mission 15's intended strategy. The one regrettable thing is that there aren't any satisfying destruction missions and that there is a lack of a long tunnel run.

# Weapons and Fighters

Compared to the previous entry, there are no longer hilariously overpowered pulse lasers or a random railgun on an F-15 for some reason, and overall, I felt that the aircraft are better balanced. Most aircraft can carry two weapons, which cuts down on the ammo available for each. I felt that most aircraft are relatively well balanced, and I was unable to find a one-size-fits-all solution. Whereas in the previous game, the SU-57 with pulse lasers was the hammer to every single nail. Most aircraft seem to have one weapon that is considered "good", and everything else is okay. QAAM missiles with guidance can do hilarious things, like making a 180 after missing the enemy, like a zealous ex, or the SASM, which explodes after missing and still kills the enemy. On the ground side, SOD has a hilariously huge range that can actually bomb the entire Megafloat from high enough up, and SFFS nukes everything in a straight line. I am happy to say I was able to clear Mission 15 with the MiG-21 and machine guns only, and use a variety of non-endgame aircraft for my Ace playthrough. I think that speaks to their versatility.

The X-40 Nightmare and FA36 are unlocked after clearing the campaign. The X-40 Nightmare is kinda weak for what they are asking, as its unique selling points are the railgun and the UAV, which act as portable QAAMs that fire lasers at the target. The issue here comes from the fact that they have a truly pathetic ammo count; carrying both means bringing a single-digit amount of special weapons. Let's cover both weapons. The railgun seems to deal a decent amount of damage, but the triple burst hurts it, as it makes it possible for one shot to miss. When dealing with bombers, where all three hits need to connect, this is kinda an issue. Meanwhile, the UAV is hilariously bad. I send them off on a journey, and they fail to kill any fourth-generation fighter.

Meanwhile, the FA36 is the second coming of Christ. It features the SASM and the SFFS, making it by far one of the best fighters by default, as it is capable of dealing with everything. There isn't much to say except that its exceptional weapon loadout, high manoeuvrability, and high speed make it the best for a lot of missions that require firepower for both air and ground targets. I guess it is fine as it is a NG+ unlock essentially, but it is ironic that this craft is the weakest enemy in game, while in the hands of the player it is the best aircraft by far. Ironically this is better than the X-40 super fighters.

# Online mode is the only issue...
As of writing, I have stopped playing online mode. For context online mode involves 4 different mode, first is a PvE where 2 teams of 4 compete to destory the enemy forces, while the s econd is a PvPvE where the 2 teams also shoot each other. The other 2 only show up occasionally in 30 minute interval, with scramble being a fight against multiple neemy aces, and emergency sortie being a boss fight against a previous series boss.

Now that I have briefly cover all modes, the first issue is the lack of variety of missions. All missions are destruction type rotating only a few maps, where the map is usually cleared within the first 5 minute. Most of them feels extremely predictable, where firstly there is a bunch of enemies, once they are defeated enemy aces will arrive, and finally a final ground reinforcement. After 3 or so runs you have essentially seen all there is to see.

The second issue I felt is that the aircraft upgrades are too powerful, the reason this is bad is that in a PvE gamemode, they will clear the board before you can do anything, while in PvPvE they are going to annilate the opposition. This is worsen by the lack of banded matchmaking, so as someone with a starting jet, I can be matched up with people using high end jets.

While I believe the online mode has potential, at its current state it is really unfun, and I am not going to go through with the grind.

# Conclusion

I feel that the game is more polished than Ace Combat 7 and serves its purpose, the missions in general are great, and the aircraft are relatively well balanced. There are no missions that are not fun, and the variety of tiny details for some missions make them fun. Unfortuatntely the post game planes feel dissapointing, I will attempt to do the machine gun challenge and no damage challenge next, which will help me get a better understanding of the game. Unfortunately the online mode damper the already great game and foundation, but is a ignorable aspect. I greatly enjoy my time here and hope to see future simillar works.
